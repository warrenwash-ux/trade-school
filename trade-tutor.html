The nine rules

These come from the Market Kings risk lessons and the Penny Stocks & Small Caps module. The tutor checks your plan against them.

  1. Risk 1% or less per tradeMany traders use 0.5% to 1% on penny stocks.
  2. No stop, no tradePick the price that proves you wrong before you enter.
  3. Reward at least twice the riskBelow 1:2, you need to win far too often to come out ahead.
  4. No tipsIf a promoter found you, you may be the exit.
  5. Check the spread and the volume firstA wide spread is a cost. Thin volume can trap you.
  6. Limit orders, not market ordersIn a thin stock a market order can fill far from the last price.
  7. Never average downAdding to a loser turns a small loss into a big one.
  8. Two losses in a day: stop for the dayWrite down what happened instead of trading to win it back.
  9. Practice first, and expect worse fills in real lifeSimulators are kinder than thin real markets.

Plain-English words

Tap through these when a word on the Plan tab is new.

Thin trading
Few shares trade and few people are waiting to buy or sell. Prices jump around, the spread is wide, and your own order can move the price. You may not be able to sell when you want to.
Bid and ask
The bid is the most buyers will pay right now. The ask is the least sellers will accept. You buy at the ask and sell at the bid.
Spread
The gap between the bid and the ask. It’s a cost you pay every time you buy and then sell.
Volume
How many shares changed hands. Average daily volume shows how easy a stock is to get in and out of.
Market cap
Share price times shares outstanding: the company’s total value on the market.
Float
The shares available for the public to trade. A small float can mean fast, violent price moves.
Dilution
A company issuing new shares, which makes each existing share a smaller slice of the company.
Limit order
An order that only fills at your price or better.
Stop order
An order that triggers when the price falls to your stop. It can fill lower if the stock gaps.
Gap
When a stock opens far above or below where it closed. A stop can’t protect you from a gap.
Catalyst
A specific, verifiable event, like an earnings report or a signed contract, that could move the price.

What this tool will never do

It won’t tell you to buy, sell, or hold. It won’t predict a price. It checks your plan against your own rules and helps you learn from the result. Real-trade plan checks are planned for later.

Educational only, not financial advice. You enter every price yourself; nothing here pulls live quotes.
